The calls HVAC contractors miss

  • No-cool calls in the heat — the homeowner calls until someone answers
  • Tune-ups and maintenance-plan visits before summer and before winter
  • Swamp cooler start-ups and shut-downs, and refrigerated-air conversion quotes
  • No-heat calls on cold nights, and the occasional gas-smell call that needs the right first instruction
  • Replacement quotes — the big tickets that go to whoever calls back first

What it does on every call

It answers with your business name, then asks for system type, what it's doing (blowing warm, not turning on, leaking, making noise), the address, whether anyone vulnerable is in the house, and the best callback number. No cooling in extreme heat, no heat in a freeze, or an elderly person or infant in the house is treated as urgent — the line offers your first emergency window and alerts you. A gas smell gets one instruction first: leave the house and call the gas company.

Routine work gets two open windows from your calendar; the caller picks one, gets a text confirmation and a reminder the day before, and you get an email with the summary.

You keep your number

Forward the calls you don't pick up to the line — on most carriers that's *72 and the number, or a setting on your office phone. Customers keep calling the number on your truck and your Google listing.

Yes. You set the rules — for example no-cool when it's over 100, no-heat in a freeze, water near electrical — and it books routine work into normal windows and flags the urgent ones to your phone right away.

It books into a Google Calendar out of the box. If you dispatch from ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or Jobber, we'll set up the cleanest handoff on the setup call.

Only prices you approve — a diagnostic fee or a tune-up special. It never makes up a number for a repair or a new system.
